# Key Insights

Format: **Finding**, why it matters, suggested action. All figures come from the cleaned dataset (7,043 customers).

## 1. Contract type is the strongest segment signal
- Churn: month-to-month **42.71%**, one-year **11.27%**, two-year **2.83%**. Month-to-month churn is about 15 times the two-year rate.
- **Why it matters:** 3,875 customers are on month-to-month contracts, and they account for 1,655 of the 1,869 churned customers.
- **Action:** offer discounts or perks to move month-to-month customers to annual plans.

## 2. The first year is the danger zone
- Churn by tenure: 0-12 months **47.44%**, 13-24 months 28.71%, 25-48 months 20.39%, 49+ months **9.51%**.
- Within month-to-month customers, first-year churn is 51.35%, and it is still 26% after four years.
- **Action:** run an onboarding programme for new customers and check in during the first 12 months.

## 3. Payment method, internet service, and tech support stand out
- Electronic check customers churn at **45.29%**, against 15% to 19% for other payment methods.
- Fiber optic customers churn at **41.89%**, against 18.96% for DSL.
- Customers without tech support churn at **41.64%**, against 15.17% with it.
- **Action:** review fiber optic service quality, encourage automatic payment methods, and bundle tech support.

## 4. Seniors churn at nearly twice the rate
- Senior citizens **41.68%** against 23.61% for other customers.
- **Action:** design a simpler support path and offers for senior customers.

## 5. Why customers say they left
- Top reasons: attitude of support person (**192**), competitor offered higher download speeds (**189**), competitor offered more data (**162**).
- Support experience and competitor offers lead the list.
- **Action:** invest in support training and review the speed and data offers against competitors.

## 6. High-risk active customers
- **950** active customers have a churn score of 70 or more. They represent **$690,811** in annual revenue and an average customer lifetime value of about $4,490.
- By contract: month-to-month 415, two-year 306, one-year 229. Month-to-month holds about **$308.8K** (45%) of the revenue at risk.
- Of the 1,869 customers who churned, 1,609 (86%) had a score of 70 or more, so the score separates the groups well in this dataset.
- **Note:** 306 two-year customers appear on the at-risk list even though two-year churn is low. A contract does not fully protect a customer, so the list should not be filtered by contract alone.
- **Action:** work the list by lifetime value, highest first.

## 7. Geography
- The most churned customers: Los Angeles **90** (of 305 customers, 29.5%), San Diego **50** (of 150, 33.3%), San Francisco 31, San Jose 29, Sacramento 26.
- Among cities with at least 30 customers, San Diego has the highest rate (33.3%) and Bakersfield the lowest (7.5%).
- **Caution:** city counts mostly reflect city size, and small cities swing widely, so use rates only for cities with enough customers.
- **Action:** start regional retention efforts in Los Angeles and San Diego.

## 8. Value of reducing churn

| Churn reduction | Annual revenue recovered |
|---|---|
| 5% | $83,479 |
| 10% | $166,957 |
| 15% | $250,436 |
| 20% | $333,914 |

This assumes a 10% reduction in churn recovers 10% of the $1,669,570 currently lost. It is a planning estimate, not a forecast.

## Limits of this analysis
- The findings show association, not cause.
- One snapshot, no trend over time.
- The risk score comes from the source data.
